A redesign is expensive if it only refreshes visuals. The best redesigns start with evaluation: what confuses visitors, what hurts trust, what limits search visibility and what blocks enquiries.

## Review first impressions

Can a new visitor understand your offer quickly? Does the design feel current and credible? Are key messages easy to find on mobile as well as desktop?

## Map the journeys that matter

Look at how people move from the homepage to services, contact, product or booking pages. Friction often hides in navigation labels, weak CTAs and pages that explain features without guiding action.

## Check SEO before you change URLs

Redesigns can support SEO when planned carefully. Audit titles, headings, content value, internal links and technical health first. Plan redirects whenever URLs change.

## Measure speed and technical health

Slow templates, heavy scripts and fragile CMS setups often travel into the new site unless you address them deliberately.

## Decide what to keep, improve or replace

Not everything needs a full rebuild. Some brands need stronger messaging and layout. Others need new information architecture, better development foundations and conversion-led page systems.
